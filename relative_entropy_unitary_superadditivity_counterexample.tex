\title{Unitary Optimization Does Not Restore Superadditivity of Quantum Relative Entropy}
\author{Ao-Xiang Liu}
\noaffiliation
\date{August 2, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
A proposed repair of superadditivity for quantum relative entropy asks
whether unitary rotations of a bipartite state and its marginals can make
the inequality valid for every full-rank reference state.  We disprove this
conjecture in the smallest bipartite dimension.  For
$\rho_{AB}=\identity_4/4$ and
$\sigma_{AB}=\diag(1,1,1,13)/16$, every allowed unitary leaves the first
argument invariant, while the superadditivity gap is exactly
$\frac14\log_2(2401/3328)<0$.  The same example also refutes the proposed
spectral inequality and the later local-unitary strengthening.  More
generally, every $m\times n$ system with $m,n\geq2$ admits an explicit
full-rank diagonal family whose optimized gap tends to minus infinity.
The failures persist on open sets.  The obstruction is therefore classical
and robust: unitary-orbit optimization cannot correct the double counting of
distinguishability generated by a correlated reference state.
\end{abstract}

\section{Problem and result}

For finite-dimensional states $\rho$ and $\sigma$, with $\sigma$ full rank,
the quantum relative entropy is
\begin{align}
 D(\rho\Vert\sigma)
 =\tr\!\left[\rho(\log\rho-\log\sigma)\right].
 \label{eq:relative-entropy}
\end{align}
If the bipartite reference state is a product,
$\sigma_{AB}=\sigma_A\otimes\sigma_B$, relative entropy satisfies the
standard superadditivity inequality
\begin{align}
 D(\rho_{AB}\Vert\sigma_A\otimes\sigma_B)
 \geq D(\rho_A\Vert\sigma_A)+D(\rho_B\Vert\sigma_B).
 \label{eq:product-superadditivity}
\end{align}
The same statement fails for a correlated reference $\sigma_{AB}$.
Zhang, Wu, and Fei proposed repairing it by optimizing the first argument
over unitary orbits~\cite{ZhangWuFei2013}: for every $\rho_{AB}$ and every
full-rank $\sigma_{AB}$, do there exist independent unitaries
$U_{AB}$, $U_A$, and $U_B$ such that
\begin{align}
 &D(U_{AB}\rho_{AB}U_{AB}^{\dagger}\Vert\sigma_{AB})
 \nonumber\\
 &\quad\geq
 D(U_A\rho_AU_A^{\dagger}\Vert\sigma_A)
 +D(U_B\rho_BU_B^{\dagger}\Vert\sigma_B)?
 \label{eq:unitary-conjecture}
\end{align}
A subsequent study reported numerical support for all two-qubit pairs and
also conjectured a stronger version in which the global rotation is the
local unitary $U_A\otimes U_B$ appearing on the right-hand
side~\cite{ZhangHeTao2014}.  These are Eq.~(4.2) of
Ref.~\cite{ZhangWuFei2013}, Eq.~(1.1) and Conjecture~3.1 of
Ref.~\cite{ZhangHeTao2014}, respectively.

We show that all these conjectures fail.  The key observation is to choose
the first argument maximally mixed.  The unitary freedom then disappears
both globally and locally, reducing the question to a determinant
inequality.  An explicit reference state reverses that inequality already
for two commuting two-qubit states, and the same construction works in every
nontrivial bipartite dimension.

\section{Flat inputs reduce the problem to determinants}

For a full-rank state $\omega$ on a $d$-dimensional Hilbert space,
Eq.~\eqref{eq:relative-entropy} gives
\begin{align}
 D\!\left(\frac{\identity_d}{d}\middle\Vert\omega\right)
 =-\log d-\frac{1}{d}\log\det\omega.
 \label{eq:flat-relative-entropy}
\end{align}
Consequently, when $\rho_{AB}=\identity_{mn}/(mn)$, the unitary conjecture
is equivalent to
\begin{align}
 (\det\sigma_{AB})^{1/(mn)}
 \leq (\det\sigma_A)^{1/m}(\det\sigma_B)^{1/n}.
 \label{eq:determinant-condition}
\end{align}
Indeed, every unitary in Eq.~\eqref{eq:unitary-conjecture} fixes the
corresponding first argument.  There is no general determinant inequality
of the form~\eqref{eq:determinant-condition}.

\begin{theorem}[Counterexamples in every bipartite dimension]
\label{thm:all-dimensions}
For every $m,n\geq2$, there are full-rank commuting states
$\rho_{AB}$ and $\sigma_{AB}$ on
$\mathbb{C}^m\otimes\mathbb{C}^n$ for which
Eq.~\eqref{eq:unitary-conjecture} fails for all choices of
$U_{AB}$, $U_A$, and $U_B$.  The violation can be made arbitrarily large.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
In a fixed product basis, let
\begin{align}
 \rho_{AB}&=\frac{\identity_{mn}}{mn},
 \nonumber\\
 \sigma_{AB}^{(\varepsilon)}
 &=\varepsilon\!\sum_{(i,j)\neq(m,n)}\ketbra{i,j}
 +h\ketbra{m,n},
 \label{eq:family}
\end{align}
where $h=1-(mn-1)\varepsilon$ and
$0<\varepsilon<1/(mn-1)$.  The reference state is full rank.  Define
\begin{align}
 a=1-n(m-1)\varepsilon,
 \qquad b=1-m(n-1)\varepsilon.
 \label{eq:ab}
\end{align}
Taking partial traces in Eq.~\eqref{eq:family} gives the marginal spectra
\begin{align}
 \operatorname{spec}(\sigma_A^{(\varepsilon)})
 &=\bigl(\underbrace{n\varepsilon,\ldots,n\varepsilon}_{m-1},a\bigr),
 \nonumber\\
 \operatorname{spec}(\sigma_B^{(\varepsilon)})
 &=\bigl(\underbrace{m\varepsilon,\ldots,m\varepsilon}_{n-1},b\bigr).
 \label{eq:marginal-spectra}
\end{align}
Both marginals are full rank throughout the stated range.  Let the
superadditivity gap be
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{m,n}(\varepsilon)
 =D(\rho_{AB}\Vert\sigma_{AB}^{(\varepsilon)})
 -D(\rho_A\Vert\sigma_A^{(\varepsilon)})
 -D(\rho_B\Vert\sigma_B^{(\varepsilon)}).
 \label{eq:gap-definition}
\end{align}
Using Eq.~\eqref{eq:flat-relative-entropy} and
\begin{align}
 \det\sigma_{AB}^{(\varepsilon)}&=\varepsilon^{mn-1}h,
 \nonumber\\
 \det\sigma_A^{(\varepsilon)}&=(n\varepsilon)^{m-1}a,
 \nonumber\\
 \det\sigma_B^{(\varepsilon)}&=(m\varepsilon)^{n-1}b,
\end{align}
we obtain the exact expression
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{m,n}(\varepsilon)
 =\frac{1}{mn}\log\!\left[
 \frac{
 \varepsilon^{(m-1)(n-1)}
 n^{n(m-1)}m^{m(n-1)}a^n b^m}{h}
 \right].
 \label{eq:all-dimensional-gap}
\end{align}
Because $(m-1)(n-1)>0$, the quantity in brackets tends to zero as
$\varepsilon\to0^+$.  Thus
$\Delta_{m,n}(\varepsilon)\to-\infty$, so the gap is negative for every
sufficiently small positive $\varepsilon$.  All first arguments in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:unitary-conjecture} are proportional to identity operators;
hence no unitary choice changes this conclusion.
\end{proof}

The theorem does more than supply a counterexample at a fixed dimension or
parameter value.  It gives a uniform construction for every $m,n\geq2$, and
its violation is unbounded as the full-rank reference approaches the boundary
of state space.

\section{An exact two-qubit certificate}

The smallest member of the family has a particularly simple rational form.
In the product-basis order $00,01,10,11$, take
\begin{align}
 \rho_{AB}=\frac{\identity_4}{4},
 \qquad
 \sigma_{AB}=\frac{1}{16}\diag(1,1,1,13).
 \label{eq:two-qubit-pair}
\end{align}
Both states are full rank, and
\begin{align}
 \rho_A=\rho_B=\frac{\identity_2}{2},
 \qquad
 \sigma_A=\sigma_B=\frac18\diag(1,7).
 \label{eq:two-qubit-marginals}
\end{align}
Using logarithms to base two,
\begin{align}
 D(\rho_{AB}\Vert\sigma_{AB})
 &=2-\frac14\log_2 13,
 \nonumber\\
 D(\rho_A\Vert\sigma_A)
 =D(\rho_B\Vert\sigma_B)
 &=2-\frac12\log_2 7.
 \label{eq:exact-entropies}
\end{align}
Therefore
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{2,2}\!\left(\frac1{16}\right)
 =\frac14\log_2\!\left(\frac{2401}{3328}\right)
 =-0.1177550074\ldots<0.
 \label{eq:exact-gap}
\end{align}
The strict sign is an integer comparison: $2401<3328$.  Equation
\eqref{eq:exact-gap} is independent of the logarithm convention.

This pair also closes the two related conjectures in the cited literature.
First, the proposed spectral inequality compares the maximum global
unitary-orbit relative entropy with the minimum marginal unitary-orbit
relative entropies.  Flat spectra make every rearrangement identical, so its
gap is again Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-gap}.  Second, replacing $U_{AB}$ by
$U_A\otimes U_B$ cannot help because the global first argument in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-qubit-pair} is invariant under every unitary, including
every local one.

For completeness, the full two-qubit family in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:family} has
\begin{align}
 \Delta_{2,2}(\varepsilon)
 =\frac14\log\!\left[
 \frac{16\varepsilon(1-2\varepsilon)^4}{1-3\varepsilon}
 \right],
 \qquad 0<\varepsilon<\frac13.
 \label{eq:two-qubit-family}
\end{align}
Hence the rational certificate is not an isolated point.

\begin{corollary}[Open sets of violations]
\label{cor:open}
The independent-unitary conjecture and its local-unitary strengthening fail
on open sets of full-rank two-qubit state pairs.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
For the independent-unitary statement, define
\begin{align}
 \mathcal{G}(\rho,\sigma)
 ={}&\max_{U_{AB}}
 D(U_{AB}\rho_{AB}U_{AB}^{\dagger}\Vert\sigma_{AB})
 \nonumber\\
 &-\min_{U_A}D(U_A\rho_AU_A^{\dagger}\Vert\sigma_A)
 -\min_{U_B}D(U_B\rho_BU_B^{\dagger}\Vert\sigma_B).
 \label{eq:optimized-gap}
\end{align}
The conjecture holds for a pair if and only if $\mathcal{G}\geq0$.  Relative
entropy is continuous when its second argument is full rank, and maxima or
minima of a continuous function over a compact unitary group depend
continuously on the states.  Thus $\mathcal{G}$ is continuous.  At the pair
in Eq.~\eqref{eq:two-qubit-pair}, it equals the strictly negative number in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:exact-gap}; the sign persists in a neighborhood.  The same
argument applies to the maximum of the locally constrained gap over
$U_A\times U_B$.
\end{proof}

\section{Discussion}

The counterexample identifies a structural obstruction rather than a
noncommutative one.  Both states commute, and the maximally mixed first
argument removes all unitary-orbit freedom.  In this setting, the conjecture
would require the geometric-mean determinant relation
\eqref{eq:determinant-condition}; the family in Eq.~\eqref{eq:family}
violates it by an arbitrarily large relative-entropy gap.  Unitary rotations
therefore cannot provide a dimension-independent repair of superadditivity
for correlated reference states.

The scope is equally clear.  The result does not affect data processing,
the standard inequality for product references in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:product-superadditivity}, or correlation-dependent
quasi-factorization bounds.  In particular, valid extensions for general
reference states introduce a multiplicative factor controlled by the
correlations in $\sigma_{AB}$~\cite{CapelLuciaPerezGarcia2018}.  Our family
shows why some such correction is unavoidable.  A natural remaining
question is to determine the optimal factor under unitary-orbit optimization
and specified spectral or correlation constraints on the reference state.
